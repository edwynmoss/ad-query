## What's new

**Group Policy, as far as the directory can tell.** Open any user or computer and the row's new Policies section draws how policy flows down to it: one line from the site through the domain and each organizational unit to the account, the policies linked at every step with their fate, blocked inheritance as a dashed rule that only enforced links pass, and what arrives in order of precedence. Enforced links, disabled links, security filtering by allow and by deny, and half-disabled policies are all worked out from the directory. A WMI filter is flagged rather than guessed at.

**A Policies register that starts with the question.** Type a person, a computer or a container and the answer comes back as a sentence ("Users in Finance get 2 policies. 4 more are linked above them but never arrive.") with the flow underneath and the rules explained in ordinary words on request. Browse the tree, which shows only the containers that carry or block policy and folds the rest, with a find box and counts fetched when you pick a container. Or list every policy: where it is linked, which half is off, who it is filtered to, which have never been edited, and which are linked nowhere, with a CSV export.

**What if.** On a policy's page, ask what would happen if it were switched off, unlinked, its link disabled or enforced. On a container's trace, ask what would happen if it blocked inheritance, stopped blocking, or one of its links were enforced or removed. The answer is a sentence ("2 users under Finance would lose Finance Lockdown") and then container by container, worked out in memory. Nothing is written to the directory.

**A person on a machine.** A signed-in session is two things at once, so trace it that way: the person's user settings beside the machine's computer settings, each with its own flow. The one thing the directory cannot show, loopback processing, is named rather than guessed at.

**Try a change on the account itself.** On a person's trace, a policy filtered by a group offers to join or leave it, and the target offers to move the account to another container. The flow redraws for that account alone and says so, since nobody else is affected. Any trace copies as plain text for a ticket.

**Drill down from anywhere.** A policy name opens the policy's own page: where it is linked, who it is filtered to, its halves, WMI filter, version and SYSVOL path. A container offers the people in it, which opens Search with the location set. A person offers their row.

**Plain LDAP stays honest.** Against a directory that is not Active Directory, the Stale accounts, Privileged access and Policies registers say they need Active Directory instead of running on data that is not there.

This is read from LDAP only. What a policy sets lives in SYSVOL and is not shown, and loopback, slow-link and WMI evaluation happen on the client.
